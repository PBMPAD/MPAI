A year with Claude, and what I've learnt

Matthew Price · 07/11/26

I've now finished six Claude Academy courses. What taught me most, though, was a year of using Claude in my own job as an associate director in a busy firm.

Where I started

I started where most people start, sorting emails in simple batches. Then I started adding documents, which was fine for simple things. Anything that needed proper interpretation was harder. Claude could read the document, but it couldn't yet do much with what I'd asked it to think about.

Getting it wrong, then getting it right

So I built trackers. A tracker is a running list that Claude keeps up to date for me. I built plenty of them and scrapped most. Every one that didn't work showed me what the next one needed.

Where it is now

Now I have a system that's highly automated, and what comes out of it is far better than where I began. It also watches for gaps, which is what I now call No Gap Working. A gap is the thing nobody is looking at: the email that got buried, the follow-up you meant to send. With the system watching, next to nothing falls between them.

The two things I couldn't do without are the voice chat and the briefs through the day. I can talk to Claude instead of typing, and the briefs tell me where things stand without me having to go and look.

The Cowork course

I did the Cowork course recently, mostly to check that the way I'd set things up matched good practice. It did, and I picked up a few tips as well. I found it reassuring that what I'd worked out by trial and error matched what the course teaches.

If you've never used it

If you run a small business and you've never used this, my advice is to keep it simple. I've rolled out processes to my own staff, and anything that wasn't extremely easy to use didn't get taken up, however clever it was. So everything I build is made to be incredibly user-friendly.

That's also how I set things up for MPAI clients. It goes on your own computer and you don't need any experience with AI. You pick the jobs you want taken off your desk, and you approve anything important before it goes out.
