A year with Claude, and what I've learnt

Matthew Price · 07/11/26

I've now finished six Claude Academy courses. But the thing that taught me most wasn't a course. It was a year of using Claude in my own job as an associate director in a busy firm.

Where I started

I started where most people start. Sorting emails, in simple batches. The basic stuff. Then I started adding documents, which was fine for simple things. Anything that needed proper interpretation was a different story. Claude could read the document, but it couldn't yet do much with what I'd asked it to think about.

Getting it wrong, then getting it right

So I built trackers. A tracker is just a running list that Claude keeps up to date for me. I built plenty of them, and most I scrapped. That trial and error was the point, though. Every one that didn't work showed me what the next one needed.

Where it is now

Now I have a system that's highly automated, and the quality of what comes out is far better than where I began. It also watches for gaps, which is what I now call No Gap Working. A gap is the thing nobody is looking at: the email that got buried, the follow-up you meant to send. With the system watching, next to nothing falls between them.

The two things I couldn't do without are the voice chat and the briefs through the day. I can talk to Claude instead of typing, and the briefs tell me where things stand without me having to go and look.

The Cowork course

I did the Cowork course recently, mostly to check that the way I'd set things up matched good practice. It did, and I picked up a few tips as well. It's reassuring to find that what you worked out by trial and error is what the experts recommend.

If you've never used it

If you run a small business and you've never used this, here's what I'd say. Keep it simple. I've rolled out processes to my own staff, and anything other than extremely easy to use fails. People don't take it up. However clever the system is, if it's awkward, it gets left alone. So everything I build is made to be incredibly user-friendly, for exactly that reason.

That's also how I set things up for MPAI clients. It goes on your own computer, you don't need any experience with AI, and you pick the jobs you want taken off your desk. You approve anything important before it goes out.
