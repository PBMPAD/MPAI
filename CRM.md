# CRM and business software: research and strategy (draft for Matthew to edit)

Written 15/11/26. Covers the six business types on the site: café or restaurant, hair or beauty salon, builder or tradesperson, estate or letting agent, dentist or clinic, shop or retailer.

## The short version

- Most small businesses don't call it a CRM. A salon's customer list lives in its booking system, a builder's in a job app, an agent's in property software, a shop's in the till. So the site asks "what software do you use?", worded for each trade.
- What decides the offer and the price is not which system is most popular. It is whether the system lets an outsider in. That splits every system into four levels (below).
- The most used systems in several trades are closed: Fresha and Booksy (salons), Tradify (trades), Alto (agents), every dental system. For those the offer is unchanged: I work from the emails and reports the system sends. That is already what MPAI sells.
- Where a system does open up (Square, Shopify, Jobber, ServiceM8, Reapit, Street, HubSpot, Xero, QuickBooks), linking it is a paid extra on top of the £1,000.
- Recommended prices: Level 1 included; Level 2 £250 per system; Level 3 quoted, from £500, with a care plan required; Level 4 (patient records and client money) never linked.

## The four levels

| Level | Plain name | What it means | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Works alongside | The system gives outsiders no way in, or charges heavily for it. I work from the emails, reports and exports it already produces. | Fresha, Booksy, Treatwell, Tradify, Alto, Jupix, OpenTable, SevenRooms, ResDiary |
| 2 | Plugs in | Claude has a ready-made link, run by the software company or listed in Claude's own directory. The owner signs in once. | Square, Shopify, Wix, Squarespace, Jobber, HubSpot, Pipedrive, Zoho, monday.com, Airtable, Xero, QuickBooks |
| 3 | Linked with extra set-up | The system can be linked, but it takes building, testing and looking after. | ServiceM8, Reapit, Street, Timely, Fergus, Epos Now, Zettle, SumUp, Lightspeed, Capsule |
| 4 | Stays outside | The system holds patient records or client money. I don't link to it, whatever it allows. | Dentally, EXACT, R4, Sensei, Cliniko, Pabau, Semble; rent and deposit handling in lettings software |

Someone with no system at all (email and a spreadsheet) is Level 1: nothing changes. If they want a customer list built, that is the existing custom CRM service, quoted as a bespoke build.

## What changes for the owner when the system is linked

The aim is that the owner never opens the system for routine admin, and never types the same thing twice.

| Job | Today (email, diary, files) | With the system linked |
|---|---|---|
| Your day, briefed | Built from inbox and diary | Also shows today's jobs, bookings or viewings, and who owes money |
| Your business, on file | Built by interview and inbox scan | Customer list and history read from the system, not rebuilt by hand |
| Paperwork ready to go | Customer details copied in from an email | Name, address, job and prices pulled from the record; the finished document noted against it |
| Invoices sent and chased | Works from a tracker sheet or the accounts package | Chases the real overdue list, and stops when payment lands |
| Meetings and calls turned into actions | Summary and follow-up email | Summary also saved to the customer's record, with follow-up tasks |
| Enquiries answered fast | Reply drafted | Reply drafted and the new lead added to the system |
| Clients kept updated | Drafted from the owner's notes | Drafted from the live status of each job |
| Monthly figures | From an export the owner saves | Pulled straight from the till or shop |

Plus one new thing the owner gets: asking in plain English. "Who's overdue?", "What's on tomorrow?", "When did we last speak to Mrs Patel?"

For Level 1 the same jobs still work. The owner gets fewer of the right-hand column, and where a regular export is needed (say a weekly appointments report), setting that routine up is one of their "jobs of your own".

## How the skills change

The skills should not be rewritten for each system. Three changes instead:

1. **One "your systems" page in the second brain.** It records which system the business uses, its level, where customers, jobs or bookings, and money owed live, and what may be read or changed. The business interview fills it in.
2. **Each skill checks that page first.** If a link exists it uses it; if not it falls back to email and files, exactly as now. invoices-chase already works this way with Xero and QuickBooks, so it is the pattern to copy into the briefs, enquiry-reply, client-updates, paperwork-draft, call-summary, booking-confirmations and monthly-figures.
3. **One short notes file per system in the master library**, starting with the Level 2 systems. The client plugin builder adds the right one, and the risk register gets a line for the linked system.

Rules for every linked system:
- Read on its own. Change only low-risk things on its own (add a note, add a new lead, add a task). Anything else waits for the owner's OK. This is the same rule as "who presses send".
- Never delete. Never move money. Never touch patient records or client-account money.
- Use the software company's own link where there is one. Avoid middlemen that pass customer data through another company.

## The offer and what it costs

Recommended. The existing prices stay as they are.

| | What the client gets | One-off | Each month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Level 1: works alongside | The standard five jobs, working from what the system sends | Included in the £1,000 | Nothing extra |
| Level 2: plugs in | The system linked, tested, rules written, and wired into their five jobs | £250 per system | Covered by Maintenance (£100) |
| Level 3: linked with extra set-up | As Level 2, but built and tested for them | Quoted, from £500 | Maintenance required, plus £50 per linked system |
| Level 4: stays outside | The admin around it | Included | Nothing extra |
| No system, wants one | A simple customer list built for them | Bespoke quote (custom CRM) | By quote |

Why these numbers:
- **£250 for Level 2** is the same figure as an extra team member, so the price list stays simple. My estimate is two to three hours: sign-in, checking what it can see, writing the rules, adding it to five skills and testing.
- **From £500 for Level 3** because the time is uncertain (my estimate: four to twelve hours) and it should be seen before it is priced. It fits the existing "jobs of your own" and Bespoke wording.
- **A care plan is required at Level 3** because a hand-built link breaks when the software company changes something, and without a plan that becomes unpaid work. The extra £50 pays for the checking.
- **Other companies' charges are the client's**, paid direct, like the Claude plan: for example Zapier, or a higher plan of their own software.

On the website this is one extra line on the pricing page, for example: "Use Square, Shopify, Jobber or similar? Linking it is £250. Others are quoted once I've seen them." The tool itself still never shows a price.

## The option on the website (built, on the branch, not live)

- Home and the contact page now ask what software the visitor uses. It is optional, and nothing is chosen to begin with.
- The question and the list follow the business type: "Your booking system" for a salon, "Your property software" for an agent, and so on. Every list ends with "Something else" (with a box to type the name) and "Nothing, just email and spreadsheets".
- Picking one shows a single plain line, by level. It never names a price, never says how the link is made, and says nothing negative about the software.
- The answer arrives in the enquiry email as a new "software" line, with the level beside it, for example "Jobber (ready-made link)" or "Fresha (works alongside)".
- The lists and wording are in solutions.js (SYSTEMS, SOFT_ASK, SOFT_LINE), shared by both pages.
- The privacy policy's list of enquiry details now includes "the software you use".

## Where to start

1. **Trades and shops first.** They have the most systems that plug in (Jobber, ServiceM8, Square, Shopify), and Xero and QuickBooks cover invoices for almost everyone else.
2. **Estate agents second.** Reapit and Street can be linked; Alto, the biggest, cannot. The email-side jobs (viewing requests, vendor updates, particulars) are strong without a link.
3. **Salons and dentists stay as email-side offers.** Do not promise links.
4. Build and test one Level 2 link end to end on MPAI's own account before selling it. Square or Xero are the obvious ones.
