Why I record and transcribe my meetings now

Matthew Price · 11/11/26

Listen to this post in my voice (made with AI)

If you record a meeting, you don't have to write anything down, so you can listen properly. The transcript then gives you a clear list of who agreed to do what, and in most meetings that's the part that goes missing.

What goes wrong when you take your own notes?

I've been sitting in meetings for 15 years, first as a graduate and now as an associate director in a busy firm. When I took the notes myself, I'd have my head down writing while the conversation moved on. I'd miss a point, or miss my chance to say something.

Other times nobody took notes at all. We'd each walk out with a slightly different idea of what had been agreed, and only find out a week later.

Where do the actions slip through?

The actions were always the worst part. Someone would say "we'll sort that" and nobody asked who "we" meant, or by when. Even when I did write things down, my scribbles often stayed in the notebook. The follow-up email I meant to send that afternoon never went.

Nobody was being lazy. It's just hard to take part in a meeting and write it all down at the same time.

What I do now

Since 09/11/26 I've recorded my MPAI calls and meetings on a Pocket. It's a small recorder that turns the conversation into a written transcript.

Claude then reads the transcript and writes a short summary with the action points. Where a follow-up email is needed, it drafts one for me to check before anything goes.

Does it work for internal and external meetings?

It works for both, in slightly different ways.

In a team meeting, nobody gets stuck as the note-taker. Everyone can join in, and the person who usually writes everything down gets to have their say for once.

On a call with a customer or supplier, you can give them your full attention. The follow-up is written from what they actually said, so you're not trying to remember it later.

Who does what, in writing

What comes out at the end is better too. Each action has a name next to it and, where one was mentioned, a date. If an action has no owner, you can see the gap straight away and ask about it, rather than finding out when it hasn't been done.

That's what I mean by meetings turned into actions. It's one of the jobs I set up for clients, so that next to nothing falls between the gaps.

Tell people you're recording

Let people know you record your calls and meetings, and make sure your privacy policy covers it.
