How a personal AI assistant gave me more headspace for work

Matthew Price · 25/11/26

Listen to this post in my voice (made with AI)

I set up Instinct, an AI assistant I text, to run the admin side of family life. It now looks after the shop, school emails, clashes and reminders. The result I didn't expect was more headspace at work, because home admin no longer pops into my head mid-morning.

What Instinct is

Instinct is an assistant you message on iMessage or WhatsApp. I connected my email, calendar and shopping sites, and it only sends, books or buys when I say yes.

MPAI is built around Claude, and Claude is still what I use and set up for business. Instinct is what I use at home.

Meta Muse has since arrived in the UK too. I've written about what Muse does, and why personal assistants like these won't run a business.

What it does for us in a normal week

We're two working parents with two young children, a day job and a business on the side. This is what it has taken off my plate:

  • Every Tuesday evening it checks what we're low on and what's on offer at Sainsbury's, suggests the next meals and builds the basket. I check the total and say yes.
  • It reads every school email and only tells me about the ones that need us to do something, like a Nativity costume needed by 01/12.
  • It spotted a work commitment on the same day as the school Nativity and drafted a note for me to approve.
  • Each evening it works out tomorrow's journeys against the electric car's range and tells me whether to charge tonight. On frosty mornings it reminds me to leave time to defrost.
  • It keeps track of the small things: return deadlines, a swimming rebooking date, and football kit to hand back before Saturday training.

Where the headspace comes from

Home admin doesn't pop into my head during the working day any more, because I know something is watching it. My evenings are mine again, now the life admin that used to fill them is mostly done. I've also stopped carrying the low-level worry of forgetting something, which I hadn't noticed until it went.

Business owners carry their home life into work, so this matters for MPAI clients too.

Why it suits a busy or ADHD brain

If you have ADHD, or a head that's always too full, these are the parts that help most:

  • I don't have to remember to check the school inbox. It brings me what needs action.
  • Reminders come when I need to act. "Leave now for swimming" is more use than "swimming tonight".
  • It does the starting. The Sainsbury's basket arrives already built, so my job is just to check it.
  • When a thought pops up, a quick text gets it out of my head and logged.

It's a practical tool, not a treatment, but it takes the remembering off you.

A gentle way into AI

Using AI for your own life is a low-risk way to get comfortable with it. You see what it's good at, where it slips, and how much to trust it, with your own shopping and diary rather than your customers. Once that clicks, using it at work feels like a much smaller step. That's been true for me, and it's why I think this whets the appetite for using AI in a business.

What didn't work

It guessed some family details from emails and got a few wrong, so now I make it label guesses and check them with me. When I asked it to list everything it was running, it found a duplicate daily check. Some of its payment options only work with US cards, so with a UK card I usually pay on the shop's own page.

Before I build anything, I'd like to hear from you. Would you want this for your home life, your business, or both?
