How to pick up a job where you left off with Claude

Matthew Price · 15/11/26

Every business I set up now gets a skill called Pick up where you left off. When you need to stop, you say "save where we are". Claude writes a short note of where the job stands and puts it in that job's folder. Days later, you or a colleague say "pick up the Smith job" and Claude carries on from the note, with no starting again.

Why I added it

I added it because I kept losing the thread myself.

Building a client's plug-in takes me several sessions over several days. Each time I opened a new chat, I spent the first part of it explaining the job again: what we had built, what we had decided and what was still waiting on the client.

Long chats cause a problem too. The longer a conversation runs, the less sharp Claude gets, and a power-user guide I read recommends starting a fresh chat when the subject changes. The trouble is that a fresh chat knows nothing about the job. With a short note, I get a clean start that still knows where the job stands.

What goes in the note?

The note is half a page, not a copy of the chat. It has the same headings every time:

  • The job: who it is for and what the work is
  • Done so far
  • Decided, with the reasons and the figures actually agreed
  • Still open, and who it is waiting on
  • The next step, in one line
  • The files the work relied on

Claude shows you the note before saving it, so you can fix anything it got wrong. It is saved in the job's OneDrive folder, next to the quotes, photos and paperwork. Older notes are never overwritten, so the folder builds up a simple history of the job.

The note is a Markdown file, which is plain text. It opens on any phone or computer, and Claude reads it better than a long, messy chat log.

How do you pick it back up?

When you say "pick up" and the job name, Claude finds the newest note in that folder. Before it carries on, it checks what has happened since: new emails from the customer, appointments in the calendar and any calls recorded on Pocket. Then it tells you where you are in three or four lines, what is new, and the next step. You decide whether to carry on.

What happens when the owner is off?

This was my other reason for adding it. If the owner is on holiday or out on site all day, someone else needs to be able to carry a job on. Usually that means a phone call, or digging through emails to work out what was agreed.

With a note in the folder, a colleague can open Claude, say "pick up" and the job name, and get the same short summary the owner would. It fits what I call No Gap Working, the idea behind MPAI that next to nothing falls between the gaps.

A plain record of decisions

The notes also give you a dated list of what was agreed and why, saved with the job. If a customer later asks why the price changed, it is there. It is not a legal record on its own, as the email or signed document behind it is what counts, but it tells you where to look.

Is there anything new to learn?

You do not need to know anything about how Claude handles long chats. Claude offers to save a note when you say you have to stop, or when a chat has run long on one job. It never saves one without asking.
