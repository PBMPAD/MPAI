Meta Muse is here: you wait ages for an AI assistant, then two come along at once

Matthew Price · 26/11/26

Listen to this post in my voice (made with AI)

Yesterday I wrote about Instinct, the AI assistant I text to run our family admin. Today it's Meta Muse, which arrived in the UK this week. Like Instinct, Muse is a personal assistant. It could help with your home life, but I wouldn't use it to run a business.

What is Meta Muse?

Muse is Meta's AI agent, an assistant that can act for you as well as answer questions. Meta launched it in the UK on 23/11/26 for anyone aged 18 or over. You use it in its own app, on the web or through WhatsApp.

It can connect to your apps, browse websites, fill in forms, send emails and buy things. Meta says it asks before it sends an email or makes a purchase. It's free, with paid plans if you want more use, and the UK launch partners include Sainsbury's, M&S, Trainline and easyJet.

Why I see Muse and Instinct as personal assistants

Both are built around one person: your inbox, your diary and your shopping. That's exactly how I use Instinct, and it's where it shines. Look at Muse's launch partners and you'll see the same thing. They're shops and travel firms, so the weekly shop and the train to see family.

I think that's the right place for them. Home admin is low risk. If it gets a meal plan wrong, nobody loses a customer.

Why a personal assistant won't run your business

This is where I'd be careful. A personal assistant is a good start, but a business needs more than one person's helper. These are the gaps I see:

  • It doesn't know how your business works. When I set up Claude for a client, I start by interviewing the owner and building a second brain, a file about their team, clients, suppliers, house rules and how they write. A personal assistant picks up your habits, not your firm's way of doing things.
  • Customer details need more thought. Before customer emails go anywhere, a business owner should know where that information is held and who can see it. An app you signed up to on your phone for the shopping isn't where I'd start.
  • Business jobs are bigger than reminders. Chasing invoices, answering new enquiries and checking nothing has slipped through the gaps each morning, midday and end of day all need setting up properly and checking.

That's why MPAI uses Claude for business, set up around how each business works, and why Instinct stays at home for me.

What about Muse for Small Business?

Meta also has a business version, launched in the US in September. It connects to many of the same apps I connect Claude to, Asana, Slack, QuickBooks and Shopify among them, and it's built around Facebook and Instagram marketing. As far as I can tell it isn't in the UK yet, and it doesn't list Outlook, OneDrive or Teams, which most of the businesses I work with run on.

Connecting the apps is the easy bit anyway. What makes it work is setting it up around how your business runs, and checking what it produces.

Where they do help a business owner

Business owners carry home life into work. If a personal assistant takes the school emails and the shopping off your mind, you get that headspace back for the business. It's also a gentle way into AI. You learn what it's good at and how far to trust it with your own diary first, rather than your customers'.

Muse against Instinct: what I'm testing

I'm now testing Muse against Instinct on the same jobs at home, like the weekly Sainsbury's shop, the school emails and diary clashes. Watch this space for a comparison post once I've given both a fair run.
