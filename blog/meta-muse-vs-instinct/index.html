A week with Meta Muse: how it compares with Instinct

Matthew Price · 03/12/26

I've now used Meta Muse for a week, on exactly the same home jobs Instinct does for me. It handled much of the same work, and on results alone it's too close to call. Where they differ is who runs them and how much of your life you hand over.

How I tested Muse against Instinct

Last week I wrote about Instinct, the AI assistant I text to run our family admin, and then about Muse arriving in the UK. To make it a fair test, I asked Instinct to summarise everything it was doing for me. Then I set Muse up to do the same list, like for like.

That meant the Tuesday Sainsbury's shop, reading the school emails and flagging only the ones we need to act on, spotting diary clashes, checking tomorrow's journeys against the car's range, and small reminders like return deadlines and football kit.

Muse took on all of it. Over a week of normal family life I couldn't pick a clear winner on results.

How is Muse different from Instinct?

I got into Instinct through an invite, and I message it on iMessage or WhatsApp. Some of its payment options only work with US cards, so I usually pay on the shop's own page.

Anyone in the UK aged 18 or over can use Muse, in its own app, on the web or through WhatsApp. It's free, with paid plans if you want to use it more.

The case for Muse

It's a name everyone knows. Most people already have WhatsApp, so there's no invite to wait for and nothing new to learn. If you're trying a personal assistant for the first time, that counts for a lot.

The case against: how much Meta gets to see

This is what gives me pause. Facebook knows what I like and WhatsApp knows who I talk to. An assistant like Muse sits in my inbox, my diary and my shopping basket. It knows when the school Nativity is, what we buy every week and where I'm driving tomorrow. That's far more personal than anything Meta has had from me before.

Meta says Muse conversations aren't shared with its advertising systems. Reports say your chats can still help train Meta's AI, and that this is switched on by default. If you use Muse, go to Settings, then Data controls, and switch it off. Instinct's terms have also been criticised for how widely it can use what you give it to train AI, so check its settings too.

Which one would I choose?

On results it's a draw. The choice comes down to who you're happy to give that much of your life to. In a business the question matters even more, because it's your customers' details as well as your own. That's why, when I set up Claude for a business, we decide where customer information goes before anything is connected.
