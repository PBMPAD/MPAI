What I did after the Microsoft outage, and how I'm future-proofing my clients

Matthew Price · 15/11/26

Listen to this post in my voice (made with AI)

Microsoft 365 went down on Thursday evening, 12/11/26, and the problems ran on through Friday. On the Friday I could still answer some emails, open SharePoint files and use Claude, but my productivity was hampered and I got far less done than normal. So I built a plan, which I call backup and switch. If one service goes down, another is ready, and I switch to it.

What happened on Friday

My work runs on Microsoft: Outlook for email, and SharePoint and OneDrive for files. Claude works from those. When Microsoft had problems I wasn't completely stuck, but everything was slower. I had four documents ready to go to clients for approval. They had to sit in a queue until the outage was over.

A small business with one set of tools has the same problem, often worse. If the one place your files and email live goes down, your day slows down with it.

My backup and switch plan

  • Files: I now use both Google Drive and OneDrive. If one fails, I switch to the other.
  • AI: I've set up a second AI. If Claude goes down, I switch to ChatGPT.
  • Later: I'm looking into open-source AI models. They run on your own computer instead of over the internet. I haven't set this up yet.

The future-proofing package for clients

I'm turning my own plan into an extra safety feature for MPAI clients. It has three parts:

  • Key files copied to both drives, so the business has everything in OneDrive and Google Drive.
  • A second AI ready to go, set up with the same instructions as Claude, so the briefs and drafts can carry on.
  • A one-page switch plan setting out what to do, step by step, when something goes down.

There's also a premium option: outage alerts that tell you when a service is down and which one to switch to.

How often does this happen?

This was a big one. Outlook, Teams, SharePoint and OneDrive were disrupted for nearly a day, around the world, including hospitals and government services. It wasn't the first this year either. An outage starting on 31/08/26 lasted about three days.
